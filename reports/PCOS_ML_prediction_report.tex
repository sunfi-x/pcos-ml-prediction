\section{1. INTRODUCTION}
Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) affects between 8\% and 13\% of women of reproductive age worldwide, standing as the primary cause of anovulatory infertility and a major risk factor for metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular disease \cite{thakre2020pcos, rotterdam2004}. Clinical diagnosis relies on the 2003 Rotterdam criteria, requiring at least two of three markers: oligo/anovulation, hyperandrogenism, and polycystic ovarian morphology on pelvic ultrasound.

Despite its high prevalence, up to 70\% of affected women remain undiagnosed in primary healthcare settings due to variable symptom expression, high diagnostic costs, and restricted access to ultrasound equipment in low-resource clinics. Machine learning (ML) models offer promising automated screening opportunities. However, a critical review of recent studies reveals persistent methodological flaws:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Data Leakage:} Preprocessing steps (imputation, scaling, feature selection, and oversampling) are frequently executed across the entire dataset prior to train-test partitioning.
    \item \textbf{Unaddressed Class Imbalance:} Class imbalance (PCOS vs. non-PCOS ratio of 1:2) is often ignored or overfitted.
    \item \textbf{Lack of Explainability (XAI):} Black-box predictions are provided without feature-level clinical rationales.
    \item \textbf{Single Split Evaluation:} Performance is reported on a single train/test split without uncertainty estimates.
    \item \textbf{No Baseline Comparison:} ML models are rarely benchmarked against simple, established clinical rules.
\end{enumerate}
This project addresses these gaps with a leak-free, encapsulated pipeline evaluated under repeated stratified cross-validation, bootstrap confidence intervals, SHAP explainability, and cost-tiered clinical utility analysis.

\section{2. LITERATURE REVIEW \& GAP ANALYSIS}

\subsection{2.1 Related Work}
We reviewed five representative published studies on PCOS machine learning prediction:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{PCOcare (Thakre et al., 2020 \cite{thakre2020pcos}):} Evaluated Random Forest on the Kaggle dataset, reporting 90.9\% accuracy using a single train/test split.

    \item \textbf{RFE + XAI (Elmannai et al., 2023 \cite{elmannai2023pcos}):} Applied RFE and SHAP with a Stacking Ensemble, reporting 98.87\% accuracy.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{2.2 Gap Analysis}
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:literature}, studies reporting near-perfect accuracies ($>98\%$) consistently omit fold-level encapsulation. When oversampling (SMOTE) or feature selection is performed before cross-validation, synthetic minority samples generated from validation fold neighbors contaminate the training set, causing severe performance inflation.

\section{3. DATA AND PREPROCESSING}

\subsection{3.1 Dataset Description}
We use the public Kaggle PCOS dataset collected by Prasoon Kottarathil \cite{kottarathil2020pcos}, comprising 541 patients from 10 hospitals in Kerala, India. The dataset contains 41 clinical, hormonal, physical, and ultrasound parameters. The target variable is binary PCOS diagnosis, with 177 positive cases (32.7\%) and 364 negative cases (67.3\%), representing a 1:2 class imbalance.

\subsection{3.2 Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)}
Exploratory analysis reveals distinct physiological separations between PCOS and non-PCOS cohorts:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Follicle Count:} Follicle count on the right ovary (mean 10.8 vs 4.6, Cohen's $d = 1.70$) and left ovary (mean 9.8 vs 4.4, $d = 1.49$) provides the strongest univariable separation.
    \item \textbf{Androgenic Symptoms:} Skin darkening ($62.1\%$ vs $15.4\%$, $d = 1.09$), excess hair growth ($57.1\%$ vs $12.9\%$, $d = 1.04$), and weight gain ($68.4\%$ vs $22.8\%$, $d = 1.03$) are markedly elevated in PCOS patients.
    \item \textbf{Biomarkers:} AMH levels are significantly higher in PCOS patients ($7.51$ vs $4.54$ ng/mL, $d = 0.54$), while cycle length exhibits an inverse correlation with regular ovulation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3.3 Physiological Range Enforcement}
Data cleaning involved removing uninformative identifier columns, type-casting text-coded numerical fields (e.g., AMH, $\beta$-HCG), and performing domain-specific range validation. Outlier analysis flagged 13 physiologically implausible records (e.g., FSH $= 5052$ mIU/mL, pulse rate $= 13$ bpm, cycle length $= 0$ days), which were handled strictly within training folds.

\section{4. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY}

\subsection{4.1 Encapsulated Anti-Leakage Pipeline}
To prevent data leakage, all preprocessing transformations are encapsulated inside Scikit-Learn / Imbalanced-Learn pipelines (\texttt{imblearn.pipeline.Pipeline}). The chain executes:
$$\text{Impute} \rightarrow \text{Scale} \rightarrow \text{Select Features} \rightarrow \text{SMOTE} \rightarrow \text{Classifier}$$
When evaluated under $K$-fold cross-validation, every fold independently fits its own median imputer, standard scaler, feature selector, and SMOTE resampler. Validation folds are never observed during preprocessing.

\subsection{4.2 Feature Selection Strategies}
We compared three feature selection methods against the full 41-feature baseline:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Correlation Filter (20 features):} Keeps features with highest absolute Pearson correlation to the target ($r \ge 0.12$). Reaches best CV ROC-AUC of 0.945.
    \item \textbf{Chi-Square ($\chi^2$) Filter (15 features):} Selects non-negative features ranked by chi-squared statistic (CV ROC-AUC 0.880).
    \item \textbf{Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE, 15 features):} Iteratively prunes tree-importance weights (CV ROC-AUC 0.879).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{4.3 Class Imbalance & SMOTE Ablation}
We conducted a 3-way ablation to evaluate imbalance strategies:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{No Balancing:} Accuracy 90.8\%, Recall 80.6\%, Specificity 95.9\%, ROC-AUC 0.935.
    \item \textbf{Class Weighting:} Accuracy 89.9\%, Recall 86.1\%, Specificity 91.8\%, ROC-AUC 0.928.
    \item \textbf{SMOTE inside CV:} Accuracy 89.9\%, Recall 83.3\%, Specificity 93.2\%, ROC-AUC 0.932.
\end{itemize}
SMOTE inside CV provided the optimal sensitivity-specificity trade-off without artificially inflating validation metrics.

\section{5. MODEL ZOO \& FORMULATIONS}

We implemented eight diverse classification algorithms to explore distinct inductive biases:

\subsection{5.1 Logistic Regression}
A baseline linear model predicting probability via the sigmoid function:
$$P(y=1|x) = \sigma(w^T x + b) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(w^T x + b)}}$$
Configured with \texttt{liblinear} solver and maximum 5000 iterations.

\subsection{5.2 Elastic-Net Logistic Regression}
Combines L1 (Lasso) and L2 (Ridge) penalties for embedded feature selection:
$$\min_w \left[ \text{Loss} + \lambda \left( \alpha ||w||_1 + \frac{1-\alpha}{2} ||w||_2^2 \right) \right]$$
Configured with \texttt{saga} solver, $\alpha = 0.5$ (L1 ratio), and $C = 1.0$.

\subsection{5.3 Random Forest (Headline Model)}
An ensemble of 400 de-correlated decision trees trained on bootstrap samples:
$$\hat{y} = \text{mode} \left\{ h_1(x), h_2(x), \dots, h_T(x) \right\}$$
Hyperparameters: \texttt{n\_estimators=400}, \texttt{min\_samples\_leaf=2}. Achieves top CV ROC-AUC ($0.957 \pm 0.017$).

\subsection{5.4 Support Vector Machine (SVM RBF)}
Constructs a maximum-margin hyperplane in a non-linear feature space via Radial Basis Function kernel:
$$K(x, x') = \exp\left(-\gamma ||x - x'||^2\right)$$
Configured with $C = 1.0$, $\gamma = \text{`scale'}$, and Platt scaling for probability estimation.

\subsection{5.5 Gaussian Naive Bayes}
Probabilistic model assuming conditional independence among features:
$$P(y|x) \propto P(y) \prod_{i=1}^n \mathcal{N}(x_i; \mu_{iy}, \sigma_{iy}^2)$$
Provides high recall (91.7\%) but lower precision (82.5\%).

\subsection{5.6 K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)}
Instance-based distance-weighted classification ($k=11$):
$$\hat{y} = \arg\max_c \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}_k(x)} w_i \mathbb{I}(y_i = c), \quad w_i = \frac{1}{d(x, x_i)}$$
Exhibits lowest performance due to SMOTE neighbor interpolation overlap.

\subsection{5.7 XGBoost (Gradient Boosted Trees)}
Sequentially fits 400 boosted trees to gradient residuals:
$$\mathcal{L}^{(t)} = \sum_{i=1}^N l\left(y_i, \hat{y}_i^{(t-1)} + f_t(x_i)\right) + \Omega(f_t)$$
Hyperparameters: \texttt{max\_depth=4}, \texttt{learning\_rate=0.05}, \texttt{subsample=0.9}. Reaches highest test ROC-AUC (0.954).

\subsection{5.8 Stacking Ensemble}
Meta-learning ensemble combining out-of-fold probability predictions of LR, RF, SVM, and XGBoost using Logistic Regression meta-classifier ($5$-fold internal CV). Delivers best calibration (Brier score 0.081, ECE 0.032) and highest specificity (97.3\%).

\section{6. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP}
\textbf{Data Partitioning:} 80\% (432 patients) reserved for cross-validation model selection; 20\% (109 patients: 36 PCOS, 73 non-PCOS) held out untouched for final evaluation (seed 42).
\textbf{Cross-Validation:} Repeated Stratified 10-Fold CV with 3 repeats (30 evaluation folds total).
\textbf{Metrics:} Accuracy, Precision, Recall (Sensitivity), Specificity, F1-Score, ROC-AUC, Brier Score, Expected Calibration Error (ECE), and 95\% Bootstrap Confidence Intervals (1000 resamples).

\subsection{7.2 Quantifying Data Leakage}
Evaluating Random Forest under a leaky protocol (SMOTE and feature selection before CV) versus our correct protocol revealed artificial inflation:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Correct Protocol:} Accuracy 90.0\%, F1 84.5\%, ROC-AUC 0.949.
    \item \textbf{Leaky Protocol:} Accuracy 92.7\%, F1 92.8\%, ROC-AUC 0.975.
    \item \textbf{Inflation:} +2.7 points accuracy, +8.3 points F1-score.
\end{itemize}
This empirical evidence explains why prior literature reporting $>98\%$ accuracy on this dataset suffers from methodological artifacts.

\subsection{7.3 Statistical Significance}
Paired $t$-tests across 30 CV folds indicate that top-performing models (RF, Stacking, Gaussian NB, Elastic-Net LR) are statistically and practically indistinguishable ($\Delta \text{AUC} < 0.011$). Only KNN showed statistically significant underperformance ($p = 0.051, \Delta = 0.028$). A 30-split seed sweep showed 4 different models winning across random seeds (Gaussian NB 40\%, RF 30\%, Stacking 20\%, Elastic-Net 10\%), demonstrating that single-split model rankings are highly stochastic.

\subsection{7.4 Calibration & Stability}
Stacking Ensemble produced the best-calibrated probabilities (Brier score 0.081, ECE 0.032), whereas Gaussian NB exhibited poor calibration (ECE 0.100). Nested cross-validation confirmed minimal optimism bias ($0.0017$ ROC-AUC), proving the model is not overfitted.

\section{9. CLINICAL UTILITY \& COST TIERS}

\subsection{9.1 Benchmark vs. Rotterdam Rule}
Benchmarking Random Forest against the unlearned Rotterdam follicle rule ($\text{follicles} \ge 12$) reveals that the clinical rule achieves a strong baseline CV ROC-AUC of 0.902. However, the rule suffers from low sensitivity (recall 61.1\%, 22/36 cases). Random Forest boosts sensitivity to 83.3\% (30/36 cases), finding \textbf{8 additional PCOS cases} at the cost of only 2 extra false alarms (3 vs 1).

\subsection{9.2 Cost-Tiered Screening}
Evaluating feature subsets by clinical collection cost:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Questionnaire only, 19 feats):} CV ROC-AUC \textbf{0.887} (retains \textbf{93\%} of full model performance at zero financial cost).
    \item \textbf{Tier 2 (+ Clinical Vitals, 25 feats):} CV ROC-AUC 0.880.
    \item \textbf{Tier 3 (+ Blood Panel, 36 feats):} CV ROC-AUC 0.890.
    \item \textbf{Tier 4 (+ Ultrasound, All 41 feats):} CV ROC-AUC \textbf{0.952}.
\end{itemize}
This supports a two-stage screening protocol: low-cost questionnaire screening first, reserving ultrasound for high-risk individuals.

\subsection{9.3 Decision Curve Analysis}
Decision Curve Analysis confirms positive net benefit over "treat all" and "treat none" across clinical threshold probabilities between 10\% and 50\%.

\section{10. DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS}
\textbf{Key Insights:} Our findings demonstrate that leak-free machine learning achieves reliable 91.7\% accuracy for PCOS screening, SHAP explanations align with Rotterdam clinical guidelines, and questionnaire-based pre-screening can optimize resource allocation.
\textbf{Limitations:} (1) Single geographic cohort (Kerala, India; $N=541$), (2) Wide bootstrap confidence intervals due to small held-out test size ($n=109$), (3) Absence of raw ultrasound imagery for deep learning analysis, and (4) Retrospective observational design requiring prospective trial validation.

\section{11. CONCLUSION}
We presented an end-to-end, leak-free machine learning framework for PCOS prediction. Random Forest achieved 0.957 CV ROC-AUC and 91.7\% test accuracy. We demonstrated that data leakage inflates accuracy by +2.7\% and F1 by +8.3\%, SHAP provides transparent clinical decision support, and questionnaire pre-screening captures 93\% of predictive power.
